### Time Difference Between New York and London (2026): What Actually Works

After five years of coordinating calls between Wall Street and the City of London, I've learned that the New York–London connection is one of the easier transatlantic puzzles—but only if you know the daylight saving time trap.

In this guide, I'll share the exact time difference, the DST dates that catch most people off guard, and the meeting windows I've found work best in practice.

### The Time Zone Basics

New York operates on Eastern Time (ET), which shifts between:
- **Eastern Standard Time (EST):** UTC-5 (November–March)
- **Eastern Daylight Time (EDT):** UTC-4 (March–November)

London uses Greenwich Mean Time (GMT) in winter and British Summer Time (BST, UTC+1) during summer months.

### The Actual Time Differences

**During Standard Time (November–March):**
London is **5 hours ahead** of New York.
- New York 9:00 AM (EST) = London 2:00 PM (GMT)

**During Daylight Saving Time (March–November):**
London is **4 hours ahead** of New York.
- New York 9:00 AM (EDT) = London 1:00 PM (BST)

### The DST Trap That Costs Meetings

Both cities observe daylight saving time, but they switch on different dates in 2026:

| City | Clocks Forward | Clocks Back |
|------|----------------|-------------|
| New York | March 8 | November 1 |
| London | March 29 | October 26 |

**Here's what this means:** For three weeks every spring (March 8–28) and one week every fall (October 27–November 1), the time difference jumps to **5 hours** instead of the usual 4.

I once had a client call scheduled for 2 PM London time on March 15. I sent the invite assuming 4 hours difference—but London hadn't switched yet. My New York team got the meeting at 9 AM instead of 10 AM. We made it work, but it wasn't pretty.

**For one-off calls:**
- 12:00 PM New York time (lunch call) = 5:00 PM London time (nearly end of day)

**Avoid scheduling:**
- Before 9:00 AM New York time (before London opens)
- After 5:00 PM London time (after New York closes)

### My Checklist Before Every Call

1. ✅ Check if it's currently DST season
2. ✅ Confirm the exact time difference (4 or 5 hours)
3. ✅ Verify the specific dates (March 8–28 and October 27–Nov 1 are the tricky windows)

### Common Mistakes I've Witnessed

**"The 3-Week Gap"**
Most people know London is 5 hours behind in winter and 4 hours in summer. But forgetting that London switches a full three weeks later than New York in spring? That's the one that ruins calls.

**"AM/PM Confusion"**
When London is at 3:00 PM, it's tempting to think New York is at 11:00 AM. But that's backwards. New York is *behind* London, so 3:00 PM London = 10:00 AM New York.

**"Friday Evening is Saturday Morning"**
An invite for "Friday 6 PM London time" sounds reasonable—until you realize that's 1 PM Friday in New York. But "Friday 8 PM London time" is 3 PM Friday New York. And "Friday 10 PM London time" is 5 PM Friday. Anything after 10 PM London is already Saturday in New York.

**Ignoring Golden Week and Chinese Holidays**

Sydney to London covers almost half the globe—17,000 kilometers and 9-11 hours of time difference. What's unique here is that both cities observe daylight saving time, but in *opposite* directions during the year.

### The Time Zone Basics

**Sydney (Australian Eastern Time):**
- Australian Eastern Standard Time (AEST): UTC+10 (April–October)
- Australian Eastern Daylight Time (AEDT): UTC+11 (October–April)

**London (GMT/BST):**
- Greenwich Mean Time (GMT): UTC+0 (October–March)

**German Holiday Blindness**
