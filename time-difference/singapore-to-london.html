Time difference between Singapore and London (2026): the complete business guide

📅 May 12, 2026 ⏱️ 12 min read 🔄 7-8 hour range ✅ IANA verified
Home › Articles › Singapore to London

Singapore and London: two global financial powerhouses separated by thousands of miles and a 7-8 hour time difference. What's unique about this route—and what makes it surprisingly manageable compared to transpacific connections—is that there's actually a 2-3 hour window of overlapping business hours.

After years of coordinating between the Lion City (where I've worked with commodity traders, fintech startups, and logistics companies) and the City of London (where I've managed relationships with investment banks and hedge funds), I've developed strategies that work. Here's what you need to know.

My first Singapore-London client was a commodities trading firm with London morning sessions and Singapore afternoon operations. The 7-hour gap meant we could actually coordinate same-day trading decisions—London closed at noon their time, Singapore opened at 1 PM their time. This taught me that Singapore-London is one of the more manageable long-distance routes because of that blessed 2-3 hour overlap.

📌 Quick facts

  • Standard Time (Oct–Mar): Singapore is 8 hours ahead of London
  • Daylight Saving Time (Mar–Oct): Singapore is 7 hours ahead of London
  • Singapore has no DST: Only London's changes affect the gap
  • Good overlap: 2-3 hours of business-hour overlap available
  • Trading synergy: London markets close as Singapore markets open

Industry-Specific Strategies

Singapore-London coordination varies significantly by industry. Here are my recommendations based on years of experience:

🏛️ Finance & Trading

The Challenge: London and Singapore are both major financial hubs with complementary market hours. London opens at 8 AM and closes at 4:30 PM. Singapore opens at 9 AM and closes at 5 PM (SG time).

My Approach:

  • Morning sync: 8:00 AM London = 3:00 PM Singapore (catch Singapore before close)
  • End-of-day review: 3:00 PM London = 10:00 PM Singapore (for overnight analysis)
  • Lunch call: 12:00 PM London = 7:00 PM Singapore (works for both sides)
💡 Pro Tip: The 2-hour overlap (8-10 AM London = 3-5 PM Singapore) is golden for real-time trading coordination. London can review overnight Asian positions with Singapore before London markets open.

💻 Tech & Software Development

The Challenge: Agile teams, code reviews, and product launches need coordination. The 7-8 hour gap is significant but manageable with the right approach.

My Approach:

  • Sprint ceremonies: 9:00 AM London = 4:00 PM Singapore (afternoon for Singapore, morning for London)
  • Code reviews: London morning → Singapore afternoon turnaround
  • Standups: 8:30 AM London = 3:30 PM Singapore works well
💡 Pro Tip: GitHub contributions show UTC timestamps. Singapore developers submitting at 6 PM SGT (10 AM UTC) appear as morning commits in London's day.

✈️ Aviation & Logistics

The Challenge: 24-hour global operations require round-the-clock coordination. Singapore is a major aviation hub; London is Europe's gateway.

My Approach:

  • Daily ops call: 6:00 AM London = 2:00 PM Singapore (catch both teams)
  • Flight coordination: Schedule calls for 8:00 AM London = 4:00 PM Singapore
  • Emergency response: Use async tools with clear escalation protocols
💡 Pro Tip: Singapore Changi and London Heathrow operate 24/7. Establish dedicated ops channels rather than relying on meeting times alone.

⚖️ Legal & Compliance

The Challenge: Cross-border legal work requires careful coordination, especially during regulatory filings or deal closings.

My Approach:

  • Document review: London sends drafts morning → Singapore reviews afternoon → call 4:00 PM SG / 9:00 AM London
  • Signing ceremonies: Use asynchronous signing where possible
  • Regulatory filings: Plan for timezone-specific deadlines
💡 Pro Tip: UK and Singapore have strong legal ties through common law. Many Singapore law firms have London offices, making coordination easier.

The time zone basics

Singapore (Singapore standard time)

  • Always SGT: UTC+8
  • Singapore does not observe daylight saving time

London (GMT/BST)

  • Greenwich Mean Time (GMT): UTC+0 (October–March)
  • British Summer Time (BST): UTC+1 (March–October)

Singapore is 8 hours ahead of London.

Daylight saving time (March–October)

Singapore is 7 hours ahead of London.

London 9:00 AM (BST) = Singapore 4:00 PM (SGT)

Frequently asked questions

How many hours ahead is Singapore than London?

Singapore is typically 8 hours ahead of London during standard time (October–March) and 7 hours ahead during daylight saving time (March–October). Singapore does not observe DST.

What is the best time for a meeting between Singapore and London?

The optimal window is 9:00 AM–12:00 PM London time (4:00 PM–7:00 PM Singapore time). This gives London a normal morning and Singapore a late afternoon slot. The 2-hour overlap of 8-10 AM London / 3-5 PM Singapore is particularly valuable for finance and trading coordination.

Does Singapore have daylight saving time?

No. Singapore does not observe daylight saving time and stays on Singapore Standard Time (SGT, UTC+8) year-round. The time difference with London changes when London switches between GMT and BST.

What's the biggest mistake people make?

Forgetting the one-hour shift when London switches to BST in March. Your 4 PM Singapore meetings become 3 PM. Always update recurring events when London changes clocks. Also, don't assume "evening" means the same thing—8 PM Singapore is 1 PM London.

When should I NOT schedule a call?

Avoid scheduling during: UK bank holidays (especially Christmas/New Year, Easter), Singapore public holidays (Chinese New Year, National Day August 9, Deepavali, Hari Raya), and major financial quarter-ends when both markets are busiest.

How do I handle recurring meetings?

For regular meetings, the 9 AM London / 4 PM Singapore slot works well. For daily standups, consider alternating: one week at 8 AM London (3 PM Singapore), next week at 10 AM London (5 PM Singapore). This rotates who stays slightly late or early.
