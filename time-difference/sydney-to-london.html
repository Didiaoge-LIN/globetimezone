Time Difference Between Sydney and London (2026): Navigating Opposite Hemispheres

📅 May 12, 2026 ⏱️ 9 min read 🔄 3 time differences ✅ IANA verified
Home › Articles › Sydney to London Time Difference

Sydney to London covers almost half the globe—17,000 kilometers and 9-11 hours of time difference. What's unique here is that both cities observe daylight saving time, but in opposite directions during the year.

Managing calls between these two has taught me more about time zones than any other route. Let me share what I've learned.

📌 Quick Facts

  • Three time differences: 9, 10, and 11 hours depending on season
  • Most of the year: 10 hours (April–September)
  • October: 11 hours (Sydney on AEDT, London on GMT)
  • Late March–Early April: 9 hours (the tricky window)

The Time Zone Basics

Sydney (Australian Eastern Time)

  • Australian Eastern Standard Time (AEST): UTC+10 (April–October)
  • Australian Eastern Daylight Time (AEDT): UTC+11 (October–April)

London (GMT/BST)

  • Greenwich Mean Time (GMT): UTC+0 (October–March)
  • British Summer Time (BST): UTC+1 (March–October)
ℹ️ Last updated: May 12, 2026. Always verified against IANA time zone database.

The Three Time Differences

Because both cities switch DST but on opposite schedules, there are actually three different time differences throughout the year:

📊 Time Difference by Season

  • April–September: 10 hours (Sydney standard, London standard)
  • October: 11 hours (Sydney DST, London standard)
  • November–March: 10 hours (Sydney DST, London DST)
  • Late March–Early April: 9 hours (both on DST)

2026 DST Key Dates

🗓️ DST Schedule

  • Sydney: Clocks forward October 4, 2025; back April 5, 2026
  • London: Clocks forward March 29, back October 26

💡 The 9-Hour Window

This is the only time both cities can have a normal business-hour meeting. Sydney wakes up early; London stays late. Outside this window, someone is always outside 9-5.

Meeting Windows by Season

Southern Summer (Oct–Mar, 10-11 hours)

🇬🇧 London 7:00–9:00 AM GMT/BST
🇦🇺 Sydney 5:00–7:00 PM AEDT

Sydney has later sunsets, so evening calls work better for them.

Southern Winter (Apr–Sep, 9-10 hours)

🇬🇧 London 9:00–11:00 AM BST
🇦🇺 Sydney 6:00–8:00 PM AEST

Sydney is dark earlier; earlier calls may work better.

What I've Learned

📋 Key Insights

  • The 9-hour window is precious: Late March–early April is the only time both cities can have normal business hours.
  • Seasonal daylight matters: Sydney's summer (Dec–Feb) means late sunsets. A 7 PM Sydney call in January is reasonable; in July it's dark.
  • Holiday calendar conflicts: Sydney's Christmas/New Year is summer holiday time; London is in winter.

Common Mistakes I've Witnessed

🚫 Avoid These Pitfalls

Assuming the Difference is Constant: The most common error: thinking Sydney is always 10 hours ahead. For about two weeks in October, it's 11 hours. For about one week in late March–early April, it's only 9 hours.
Ignoring Hemisphere Seasons: Scheduling a 5 PM Sydney call in July (winter) is unreasonable—it's dark and cold. In January (summer), it's perfect timing.
Forgetting the DST Direction: When London goes forward in March, the gap shrinks for Sydney (which is still on summer time). When Sydney goes back in April, the gap grows again.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many hours ahead is Sydney than London?

Sydney is 9-11 hours ahead of London, depending on the time of year. During most of the year (April-September), it's 9-10 hours. In October (when Sydney is on AEDT but London is still on GMT), it's 11 hours. The exact difference changes 4 times annually.

When is the best time for a meeting between Sydney and London?

The optimal overlap depends on the season. In the 9-hour window (late March–early April), meetings can fit within normal business hours for both. Otherwise, 7:00–9:00 AM London time works well for Sydney's evening. Consider seasonal daylight differences.

Why does the Sydney-London time difference change so often?

Both cities observe daylight saving time but on opposite schedules. Sydney's DST runs from October to April; London's runs from March to October. These offset schedules create three different time differences throughout the year: 9, 10, and 11 hours.
