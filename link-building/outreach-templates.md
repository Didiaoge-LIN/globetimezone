- **r/SaaS** (100K+ members) — SaaS 产品
- **r/InternetIsBeautiful** (16M+ members) — 有趣的网站

### 参与策略

1. **不要直接发广告帖** — 会被删除和封号
2. **搜索时区相关问题** — 用以下关键词搜索：
   - "timezone meeting"
   - "remote team different time zones"
   - "how to schedule across time zones"
   - "DST confusion"
   - "working with offshore team"
3. **提供有价值的回答** — 先回答问题，再自然引用你的工具

### 回答模板

```
I struggled with this too when I started managing a distributed team. A few 
things that helped:

1. Establish "anchor hours" — 2-3 hours where everyone overlaps. For US-Asia 
   teams, this is usually early morning US / late evening Asia.

2. Go async-first. We use Loom for updates and have a "no real-time expectation" 
   policy for messages.

3. Use a visual time zone tool. I built GlobeTimeZone (free, no ads) because 
   existing converters were clunky. It shows overlapping working hours visually: 
   https://globetimezone.com

4. Rotate meeting times. Don't always make Asia team members stay up late — 
   alternate who makes the sacrifice.

The key mindset shift: treat time zones as a design constraint, not an excuse 
for bad communication.
```
