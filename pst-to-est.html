PST to EST Time Difference & Conversion (2026 Updated)

I run cross-country US remote teams every week, and the PST-EST 3-hour gap is by far the most commonly messed-up time zone in American business. West Coast morning meetings become East Coast lunch hours, and late West Coast afternoons hit East Coast evenings fast. I've seen countless client calls and team stand-ups get rescheduled because of simple PST-EST mix-ups.

This guide covers the exact time gap, complete 24-hour conversion table, 2026 daylight saving rules, and the most practical meeting windows for US-wide teams.

Exact Time Difference Between PST and EST

  • Standard Winter Time: Eastern Time (EST) is 3 hours ahead of Pacific Time (PST). When it's 9:00 AM in Los Angeles (PST), it's 12:00 PM in New York (EST).
  • Daylight Saving Summer Time: Eastern Daylight Time (EDT) is still 3 hours ahead of Pacific Daylight Time (PDT). When it's 9:00 AM PDT, it's 12:00 PM EDT.

Last updated: May 14, 2026. Cross-checked with official IANA time zone data.

Quick Real-World Facts

  • If it's 9 AM in Los Angeles (PST/PDT), it's always 12 PM in New York (EST/EDT), all year round.
  • A 3 PM PST team call is 6 PM EST — late afternoon East Coast, mid-afternoon West Coast.
  • Core business overlap: 9 AM-4 PM PDT / 12 PM-7 PM EDT is the gold standard for cross-US corporate meetings.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is PST always 3 hours behind EST?

Yes. The three-hour gap never changes, even during daylight saving time. PDT stays exactly three hours behind EDT all year long.

Q: What is the best cross-US meeting window between PST and EST?

9 AM-4 PM Pacific Time / 12 PM-7 PM Eastern Time is the ideal shared working window for US-wide teams.

Q: Do Pacific and Eastern Time switch DST on different dates?

No. All US time zones follow the same national daylight saving schedule, so the offset never shifts between these two zones.
